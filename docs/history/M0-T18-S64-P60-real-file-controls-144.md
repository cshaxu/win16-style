# M0 T18 S64 P60: real file controls at 144 DPI

The previously audited author samples PhotoAlbum (actual VB5/MSVBVM50) and Compress-Z-It (VB6/MSVBVM60) were copied to private research directories and given per-monitor DPI-aware manifests; their original files remain unchanged. A DPI-aware native observer and same-thread project-owned witness ran each bare and through the P59 published package. These optional samples and dependencies are not shipped or required by the project. This is a 144-DPI real-runtime cross-check of existing support, not a new renderer change.

VB5: both runtime-owned FileListBox and DirListBox were observed at 144 DPI. In the styled instance their attachments were present, with respectively two and five System-font text records, versus native-font records without the launcher. Source font handle, window geometry and selection remained unchanged and both jobs exited normally. Two self-owned BMP files populated the private directory. The first styled attempt had no matching image files, so its file list was empty and the observer correctly failed its text-record assertion; that attempt is not a pass. The scratch-only observer intentionally excluded DriveListBox; its inherited `drive_escape=1` summary is inaccurate and must be disregarded. No drive dropdown or drive switch was tested.

VB6: actual runtime-owned FileListBox and DirListBox both entered the styled System-text route at 144 DPI; bare output remained native. The observer sent End and Home to the file list, received parent notifications, restored selection, and the target, same-thread hooks, events and job cleaned up normally. Source font and geometry remained unchanged. The private sample manifest additionally retained its existing registration-free COM declarations. The two real samples were x86; synthetic messages do not prove physical input. Other DPI values, arbitrary filenames/languages, directory navigation and all deferred DriveListBox/OCX work remain outside this evidence.

The original VB5 EXE SHA-256 is `c3c28e74957dff6d2409fffd09c5070701d3d02ca9a6dbfae521df4f826984d3`, and the original VB6 EXE is `36728710ef7fb5f3a508b244b041498c13af75c8a30fd9c94eb50ddd19deccbe`; the scratch copies differ because of their embedded DPI manifests. Their provenance and original notices are recorded in `docs/etc/evidence/vb-file-controls.json` and `vb6-file-controls.json`.

An author-provided VB5 database tutorial link was also investigated: its advertised download host did not resolve, and three public alternate addresses returned HTTP 404. This does not establish that VB5 Data is impossible; no VB5 Data program was obtained or executed. No product source or binary changed. Both published locations still match the four P59 SHA-256 values. T18 and S64 remain open.
